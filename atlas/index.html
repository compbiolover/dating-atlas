How the estimates work

We start with everyone who lives in the metro and narrow it down, one step at a time, to the people who could realistically be a match for you:

  1. People of the genders you date, in your age range
  2. who are single
  3. who are looking for dates, and for the kind of relationship you want
  4. who have a degree, if you asked for one
  5. who are open to dating someone of your gender
  6. who share your politics, faith and diet
  7. whose plans for kids fit yours
  8. and who you'd click with, both ways.

Most steps use data for each metro. Who's looking, who's open to you and whether it clicks use national surveys, so they're the same everywhere. Climate and rent don't change the count; they only filter which metros you see. Open a topic to see where each number comes from.

Who lives there

Population comes from the Census Bureau's 2025 estimates for all 387 metros (via Wikipedia's metro list). The Census only counts women and men, so we assume 1.5% of adults are nonbinary, close to Pew's estimate for adults under 50.

Who's single

The Census Bureau's American Community Survey (2019 to 2023) counts people in each metro who never married, are divorced or are widowed. Some of them live with a partner, so we take those out: about 1 in 4 single women in their late 20s, for example.

Degree rates come from the same survey. Single people hold degrees a little less often than others their age, so we adjust for that. Small metros have less precise numbers.

Who's looking

Not every single person wants to date. In Pew's surveys, 20% of singles are already in a committed relationship. Of the rest, about half are looking under 40. After 40, about 45% of men but only 27% of women are looking, and after 65 far fewer.

If you want something serious or something casual, we also take out people who only want the other.

Who's open to you

Gallup's 2024 survey on sexual orientation tells us who dates which genders: about 94% of men and 14% of women date women, for example. These rates are national, so they miss cities with larger LGBTQ+ communities.

Politics and faith

Politics starts from how each metro voted in 2024. A large national survey, the 2024 Cooperative Election Study, shows how single people aged 25 to 35 describe their views in places that voted different ways.

Faith comes from PRRI's county estimates of religion. Politics and faith go together: progressives are more often non-religious, conservatives more often Christian. We account for that rather than treating them as separate. The religion figures cover all adults, not just singles, so they're rougher for groups that skew young or old.

Diet

About 4% of Americans are vegetarian and 1% vegan (Gallup 2023), more among women and liberals. Kosher and halal depend on how many Jewish and Muslim people live in the metro.

Kids

We take out people whose plans clash with yours. Among young adults without kids, about half want them and 30% aren't sure (Pew). People who aren't sure always stay in.

If you say when you want kids, the National Survey of Family Growth lets us take out people on a very different timeline. The share who already have kids comes from the Census Bureau and misses some parents, mostly fathers.

Whether it clicks

In speed-dating studies, people want to see about 4 in 10 partners again. A yes there only means another conversation, so we use a bit less: 35% for men and 25% for women. You can change both rates.

Your traits change how many people are interested in you: your age (bigger age gaps put more people off), your height (between women and men), whether you have a degree or a job, and whether you have kids. These come from studies of mostly straight couples, so they're rougher for everyone else.

On the apps

In Pew's 2022 survey, 28% of singles who were looking used a dating app or site, and LGB singles about 1.5 times as often. The circles on the map show this number.

Competition

Competition compares the people you're looking for with everyone else who's looking for them. Below 1 means there are more people looking than people to find. Gender balance is simpler: single, looking women per single, looking man.

How sure is the number

No input is exact. We rerun the estimate 2,000 times, nudging every input by how uncertain it is, and show the middle 80% of the results. The page also names the input that matters most. The range doesn't cover mistakes in how the steps fit together.

Rent and climate

Rent is what a recent mover pays for a 1-bedroom, as a share of typical pay for someone with your education (Census Bureau, 2019 to 2023). Over 30% counts as a heavy burden. Rents have risen since, so treat it as a floor.

Climate uses NOAA's average January temperature, 1991 to 2020: warm winters are 50°F or above, cold ones 30°F or below. Neither rent nor climate changes your number. They only filter which metros you see.
